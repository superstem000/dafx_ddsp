Fitness-Distance Correlation (FDC, quantile-binned)
Spearman correlation between a point's loss value and its distance to the optimum. Positive FDC means lower loss reliably indicates 
proximity to the answer. We use quantile bins (equal number of samples per bin) to measure whether the correlation holds at all 
distances, avoiding the empty-bin problem in high dimensions.
— Jones & Forrest (1995). "Fitness Distance Correlation as a Measure of Problem Difficulty for Genetic Algorithms." ICGA.

Autocorrelation Length (Ruggedness)
Random walk through parameter space; compute autocorrelation of the loss sequence. The lag at which autocorrelation drops below 
1/e is the "grain size" of the terrain. If this is smaller than the optimizer's population spread, the optimizer can't distinguish 
signal from noise.
— Weinberger (1990). "Correlated and Uncorrelated Fitness Landscapes and How to Tell the Difference." Biological Cybernetics.

Information Content (Entropy)
Along a random walk, classify each step as "up", "down", or "flat". Compute Shannon entropy of this sequence. 
Low entropy = terrain dominated by one category (e.g., mostly flat plateaus). High entropy = terrain that actively responds to 
parameter changes in all directions.
— Vassilev et al. (2000). "Information Characteristics and the Structure of Landscapes." Evolutionary Computation.

Meta-model Linear Fit (R² and Slope)
Fit a linear regression of loss vs distance-to-GT across random samples. R² measures how well a simple linear slope explains the 
landscape — high R² means consistent gradient direction. The regression slope measures the magnitude of that gradient — how much 
loss actually changes per unit distance.
— Mersmann et al. (2011). "Exploratory Landscape Analysis." GECCO. Directly from the meta-model feature set.

y-Distribution (Skewness, Kurtosis)
Statistics of the sampled loss values themselves, regardless of location. Skewness measures asymmetry — right-skewed means most 
of the space has similar loss with a few extreme outliers. Kurtosis measures tail heaviness — high kurtosis means occasional 
extreme loss values far from the mean.
Skewness: most values low, high loss outliers (positive) / most values high, low loss outliers (negative)
          'more positive' = high loss outliers are more extreme
Kurtosis: number of outliers
— Mersmann et al. (2011). "Exploratory Landscape Analysis." GECCO. Directly from the y-distribution feature set.

Monotonicity
Walk in a straight line from a random point toward the ground truth. Fraction of steps that decrease the loss. Directly answers: 
"if the optimizer moves toward the correct answer, does the loss consistently reward it?"
— Our adaptation, inspired by FDC (Jones & Forrest, 1995).
